    Playback & practice

    Level presets select patterns for you.

    A little rhythm, every day

    1. Pick a level. Level 0 starts with long notes. Each level adds a rhythmic challenge; level 10 mixes syncopation, ties, and irregular tuplets. Level 11 adds 32nd notes. Level 12 adds mixed 32nd figures, dotted sixteenths, and uneven tuplets. Levels 5+ can randomize meter, and 9+ can randomize tempo; turn either off in Customize.

    2. Listen. The snare guide plays every note attack. Use Snare on/off beside the metronome to mute it while keeping your hit clicks. Your metronome sound marks the beat, with an accented downbeat.

    3. Practice. After the count-in, press any regular key on Computer, or use the tap pad or score on Mobile, at each note’s start. Long notes need one tap. Rests and tied continuations need none.

    Pause. Use Pause/Resume beside Listen to hold your place mid-round. Tap Resume for a 3–2–1 countdown above the score before continuing.

    Timing. Hits allow up to ±160 ms, narrowed for tightly spaced notes. Each note owns a timing slot, divided halfway between neighboring attacks. Your first tap must be inside its hit window. Extra taps in that slot forfeit the note, even after a successful hit. Early taps cannot be rescued by tapping again. Accuracy counts hits, misses, and extra taps. Input offset can compensate for consistent device delay. Bluetooth audio can add delay.

    Reading the score. Beams follow each rhythmic group. Tuplet numbers mean that many notes fit into the displayed group. Dots add half the written duration. Curves connect tied notes; play only the first. In practice, rests turn green when their full written duration is over, including yellow trap rests. Listen mode leaves rest colors unchanged. Tapping a yellow trap rest forfeits the next note and shows a warning. Beat counts and the moving-bar hint are toggles. Scrolling always follows. With the moving-bar hint off, the bar pulses at each measure and fades out. Hitting the start of a tie colors its continuations and curves green at 50% opacity. Missing it colors them red. Beam and tuplet bracket segments follow their own notes. Tuplet numbers turn green or red when every playable note is hit or missed.

    Polyrhythms. Both lanes fill one bar. Use J for the upper lane and K for the lower lane, or change the keys in Customize. On Mobile, use the two pads or tap the upper/lower score areas. Simultaneous notes need both inputs. Customize offers presets and custom pulse counts.

    Compound meter. In 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8, BPM is a dotted-quarter beat. In 5/8 and 7/8, BPM is an eighth note, with 2+3 and 2+2+3 grouping. Other meters use quarter-note BPM.

    Modes. Impossible adds two bars to the base length, rolls a level from 10–12 each new rhythm, smoothly rewinds the moving bar for two seconds, and pays 2.5× normal gold with Modded on. Perfect runs add a 500 gold bonus. Its score settings are locked; count-in, base bars, and Playback & practice remain adjustable. Endless rises and eases in waves while slowly getting harder. Rhythm Maker inserts notes or rests when you press a value button. Left and right move the insertion cursor; Delete removes the previous entry. Tap tempo and time signatures to edit them. Add markings only at barlines. Empty space plays as rests. Modded gold grows by 15% per level before mode multipliers. Strange uses explicit tuplet ratios with uneven values across beat boundaries. A perfect 100% run plays the victory sound; Modded adds its special celebration and bonus gold.

    Everything is embedded in this file, including all six supplied sounds. No internet connection is needed.
